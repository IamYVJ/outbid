    OUTBID — Rules

    Players: 2 or more · Strategy · Resource management · Simultaneous bidding

    Objective

    Be the first player to win Y rounds within a maximum of X rounds, while managing your limited volume. The highest bidder wins each round, but every player loses the volume they bid, win or lose.

    Setup

    • Starting Volume (V) — given equally to each player.
    • Maximum Rounds (X) — the game's round limit.
    • Target Wins (Y) — between 1 and X.

    Each round

    1. Choose — everyone secretly picks a whole-number bid, 0 up to their remaining volume.
    2. Reveal — all bids are shown at once.
    3. Winner — the highest bid above zero wins. If two or more players tie for the highest bid, nobody wins. Zero never wins.
    4. Deduct — every player loses exactly what they bid.
    5. Continue — until someone has Y wins or X rounds have been played.

    Winning

    The first player with Y round wins takes the game immediately. If X rounds pass and nobody reached Y, the match is a draw.

    Core principle

    Winning a round is not always the best move. In OUTBID, winning the battle can cost you the war.

    The Science Behind the Strategy

    A game of strategic decisions, finite resources, and competitive intelligence.

    OUTBID is best described as a finite-horizon, budget-constrained, repeated all-pay auction: everyone pays their bid whether or not they win.

    Game theory

    • All-pay auctions — every player sacrifices their bid, even when they lose.
    • Nash equilibrium & mixed strategies — if you always bid the same way, opponents can exploit you. Randomising makes you harder to predict.
    • Strategic interdependence — a bid is only good or bad relative to what others bid.
    • Backward induction — reason from the last round backwards.

    Economics

    • Scarcity & budget constraints — volume is finite.
    • Opportunity cost — volume spent today can't be spent later.
    • Marginal analysis — is one extra unit worth the edge it buys?

    Behavioural economics

    • Sunk cost — don't bid more just to justify earlier bids.
    • Loss aversion & overbidding — fear of losing can push spending beyond what is strategically justified.
    • Regret & learning — compare what you did with what you could have done, and adapt to revealed behaviour.

    Related model: Colonel Blotto

    Blotto players split resources across battlefields at once. OUTBID makes the allocation round by round, so you can react to outcomes: related, but not identical.

    Worked example

    Two players, 100 volume each, 3 rounds, 2 wins needed. A bids 70 and beats B's 20 — but A has 30 left. B (80 left) can bid 31 in both remaining rounds and take the game. Tactical success is not strategic success.

    Learning objectives

    1. Strategic interdependence
    2. Resource optimisation
    3. Opportunity cost
    4. Strategic uncertainty
    5. Recognising behavioural biases
    6. Planning across rounds
